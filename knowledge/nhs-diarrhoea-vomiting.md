Causes of diarrhoea and vomiting. They are most often caused by a stomach bug or food poisoning. Diarrhoea can also come from certain medicines, food intolerance or allergy, irritable bowel syndrome, anxiety, appendicitis, inflammatory bowel disease, coeliac disease or diverticular disease. Vomiting can also be caused by pregnancy, migraine, motion sickness, inner ear infections, certain medicines, or infections such as a urinary tract infection.

Self-care. Most cases can be managed at home. Rest and drink plenty of fluids in small sips. Eat when you feel able, and avoid fatty or spicy foods. Paracetamol can ease discomfort. Avoid fruit juice and fizzy drinks. Do not give anti-diarrhoea medicine to children under 12, and do not give aspirin to children under 16. Diarrhoea usually lasts 5 to 7 days and vomiting 1 or 2 days. Stay off school or work until you have been free of symptoms for at least 2 days, and wash hands and laundry carefully to limit spread.

Signs of dehydration include dark, strong-smelling urine and passing urine less often than usual. In children under 5, fewer wet nappies is also a sign.

Speak to a pharmacist if you or a child over 5 show signs of dehydration, if you are older or have a weakened immune system, or if you want short-term relief from diarrhoea. A pharmacist may suggest oral rehydration powder.

Call the NHS 111 service if you are worried about a baby under 12 months, a child stops feeding, a child under 5 shows signs of dehydration, you cannot keep fluids down, there is blood in the diarrhoea, or diarrhoea lasts more than 7 days or vomiting more than 2 days.

Call emergency services or go to A&E for vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds, green vomit in adults or yellow-green or green vomit in children, possible poisoning, a stiff neck with sensitivity to light, a sudden severe headache or tummy ache, blue, grey or pale skin, lips or tongue, severe difficulty breathing, or confusion. Do not drive yourself, and bring any medicines you take.
