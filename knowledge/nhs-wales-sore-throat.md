Causes of sore throat. Most sore throats are caused by viruses such as colds or flu, and smoking can also cause them. Bacteria are a less common cause. Other conditions that can cause a sore throat include laryngitis, tonsillitis, strep throat and glandular fever.

Sore throats are common and usually harmless, and they normally get better by themselves within a week.

Self-care for sore throat. Gargle with warm salt water and spit it out afterwards (not for children). Drink plenty of fluids. Eat cool or soft foods. Avoid smoke. Sucking ice or hard sweets may soothe the throat, but keep small hard items away from young children. Rest. A pharmacist can suggest pain relief such as paracetamol or ibuprofen, medicated lozenges or sprays. Antibiotics are not usually needed because they rarely ease symptoms or speed recovery.

See a GP about a sore throat if a child under 5 needs help, if the sore throat has not improved after a week, if sore throats keep coming back, or if there is a lump in the mouth or neck or a mouth ulcer lasting more than 3 weeks.

Get an urgent GP appointment or contact the NHS 111 Wales service if you are worried about your symptoms, you have a very high temperature or feel hot, cold or shivery, you have signs of dehydration such as passing less urine or dark urine, or you have a weakened immune system, for example from diabetes or chemotherapy.

Call emergency services or go to A&E if breathing is difficult, you cannot swallow, there is drooling, there is a high-pitched breathing sound (stridor), or symptoms are severe and getting worse quickly. Do not drive yourself to A&E, and bring any medicines you take.
