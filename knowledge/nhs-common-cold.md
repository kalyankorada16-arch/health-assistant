Symptoms of a common cold. Cold symptoms usually build up over 2 to 3 days. The main ones are a blocked or runny nose, sneezing, a sore throat, a hoarse voice, a cough, and feeling tired and unwell. Some people also get a high temperature, aching muscles, loss of taste or smell, or pressure in the ears and face. Flu is usually more severe and comes on within hours.

Self-care for a cold. Rest, drink plenty of fluids, and eat well. A warm lemon and honey drink or gargling with salt water (not for children) may ease a sore throat. Breathing in steam may ease a blocked nose. Stay at home and avoid others if you have a high temperature or cannot manage normal activities. Do not give aspirin to children under 16. Do not give honey to babies under 12 months. A pharmacist can advise on pain relief, decongestants (not for children under 6) and nasal sprays (not for more than a week), and can check that cold medicines do not overlap with paracetamol or ibuprofen. Antibiotics are not recommended because colds are caused by viruses.

See a GP about a cold if you have a high temperature lasting more than 3 days, symptoms that get worse or do not improve after 10 days, a very high temperature or feel hot, cold or shivery, a cough lasting more than 3 weeks, shortness of breath or chest pain, a long-term condition or weakened immune system, or any worry about a child's symptoms. If symptoms are severe or you feel seriously unwell, seek urgent help rather than waiting for a routine appointment.
