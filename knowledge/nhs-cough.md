Causes of cough. Most coughs are caused by a cold or flu. Other causes include smoking, heartburn (acid reflux), allergies such as hay fever, infections such as bronchitis or COVID-19, and mucus dripping from the back of the nose into the throat. A cough is rarely a sign of something serious such as lung cancer.

Self-care for cough. Most coughs clear up by themselves within a few weeks. Rest, drink plenty of fluids, and stay at home away from other people if you have a fever or feel too unwell for normal activities. Paracetamol or ibuprofen can ease pain. A hot lemon and honey drink may help, though evidence is limited. Check medicine leaflets or ask a pharmacist first, because some remedies are not safe for everyone, including pregnant women and young children. Cough syrups and sweets may reduce coughing but will not stop it.

See a GP about a cough if it lasts more than three weeks, if you are losing weight without a clear reason, or if your immune system is weakened, for example by chemotherapy or diabetes.

Get urgent help for a cough (an urgent GP appointment or the NHS 111 service) if the cough is severe or getting worse quickly, you feel very unwell, you have chest pain, you have a painful swollen area on the side of your neck, you struggle to breathe, or you cough up blood.
