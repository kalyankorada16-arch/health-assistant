What counts as a high temperature. A high temperature, also called a fever, is usually 38 degrees Celsius or above. Signs include feeling hot, cold or shivery, sweating, or your chest or back feeling hotter than usual. You can have a high temperature even if a thermometer reads below 38 degrees Celsius, because normal temperature varies between people and during the day.

Self-care for a high temperature. Rest. Drink plenty of fluids, with water being best, to avoid dehydration; your urine should be light yellow and clear. Take paracetamol or ibuprofen if you feel uncomfortable. Stay at home and avoid contact with other people until your temperature has come down.

Get urgent advice from a GP or the NHS 111 service if a high temperature is not improving after home treatment or is getting worse. If you think someone's life is at risk, call emergency services immediately. If you are unsure what to do, NHS 111 can advise you.
