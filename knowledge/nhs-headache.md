Causes of headache. Common causes include colds and flu, stress, heavy drinking, poor posture, eyesight problems, skipped meals, dehydration, taking too many painkillers, and hormonal changes linked to periods or menopause. Migraine and cluster headaches can cause severe headaches. Keeping a headache diary can help you spot your triggers.

Self-care for headache. Drink enough fluids, rest if you also have a cold or flu, and try to relax. Paracetamol or ibuprofen can help. If you have a high temperature or feel too unwell for normal activities, stay at home and avoid other people. Avoid skipping meals, sleeping more than usual, long periods of screen use, and alcohol.

See a GP about a headache if it is not improving with self-care or is getting worse, or if you get headaches regularly.

Get urgent advice (an urgent GP appointment or the NHS 111 service) if you or a child has vision or eye problems, headaches triggered or worsened by coughing, sneezing, bending or exercise, vomiting with the headache, jaw pain when eating, a sore or tender scalp, or any other symptom that worries you. A child's headache that is getting worse or wakes them at night also needs urgent advice.

Call emergency services or go to A&E for a headache with a seizure, numbness or weakness in the face or body, a sudden onset of extreme pain, a head injury in the last 3 months, difficulty speaking, balancing, walking or remembering things, drowsiness or confusion, loss of vision, a rash that does not fade under a glass, or a very high temperature with a stiff neck or sensitivity to light. Do not drive yourself to A&E, and bring any medicines you take.
